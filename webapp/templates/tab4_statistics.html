A strategy of drawing a hard perimeter around two or three "worst" neighbourhoods would be built on noise rather than signal — the per-capita view on the Exploration tab is the better basis for targeting prevention, because it corrects for how many people actually live in each area.

Feature correlation

df.corr() across the numeric features.

{% if max_corr is defined %}

What predicts an arrest?

Nothing numeric does. The largest absolute correlation anywhere in the matrix is {{ max_corr }}. Whether a crime ends in an arrest is essentially uncorrelated with the year, the month, the hour of day or the coordinates.

This is a useful result rather than a dead end: the arrest outcome is driven by what kind of crime it is — a categorical variable that cannot appear in a Pearson matrix at all. A model built to predict arrest likelihood from time and place alone would fail.

{% endif %}

Where the signal actually is

Dimensions ranked by coefficient of variation.

Recommendation to the CPD

Ranking every dimension by how much it actually moves crime volume settles the resourcing argument: hour of day carries several times the signal of any calendar dimension, and community area is the strongest geographic one.

Build the patrol model on the clock first and the map second. Rostering by day of week, or waiting for a year-on-year trend to appear, chases variation that is statistically indistinguishable from noise.
